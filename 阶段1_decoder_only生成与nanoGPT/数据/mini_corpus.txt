In a small language model, every next symbol is a prediction.
The model reads a short context and assigns a score to every possible next character.
Training compares those scores with the character that actually comes next.
Generation chooses one character, appends it to the context, and repeats the loop.

The decoder can read the past, but it must not read the future.
A causal mask makes this rule visible in the attention matrix.
The same rule is used during training and during generation.
Training can process many known positions at once; generation waits for each new token.

A tokenizer turns text into IDs, and an embedding turns IDs into vectors.
The vectors pass through attention, a small feed-forward network, and a final vocabulary head.
Logits are scores rather than text, so a sampling rule is needed at inference time.
Greedy decoding is simple, while temperature and nucleus sampling change the variety of choices.

The cache stores keys and values from earlier positions.
It does not remove the autoregressive dependency, but it avoids recomputing unchanged history.
Good experiments record the prompt, the seed, the model size, the loss, and the stopping rule.
Small experiments explain mechanisms; they do not prove broad language ability.

In this study project, a clean data flow is more important than a large model.
We inspect shapes, compare two implementations, and keep the limits of each experiment visible.
